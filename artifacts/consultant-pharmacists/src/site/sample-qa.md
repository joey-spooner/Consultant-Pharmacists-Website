# Sample Q&A: Calcium (Chapter 5 self assessment)

Source: `Sample Self Assessment - Calcium.rtf`, from Dr. Baumgartner. Each answer follows its question.

Two spelling corrections were made to his text and need his OK: "Chovstek" is now "Chvostek" (item 31) and "anerobically" is now "anaerobically" (item 20). The same misspellings are in the book itself, so the book and this sample will differ until the book is fixed.

1. Calcium is mobilized from the bone by two hormones, _____________ and __________ to maintain homeostasis of plasma levels of calcium.\
**Answer:** parathyroid hormone  (PTH, parathormone), vitamin D

2. The action of ___________ is to antagonize parathormone and inhibit bone resorption by lowering both serum calcium and phosphate.\
**Answer:** calcitonin

3. _________ (mineral) and _______ factor are intimately associated with at least one major route of B12 absorption from the distal ileum.\
**Answer:** calcium and intrinsic

4. The mediation of a protein (using vitamin K) called ___________ in hypercalcemic, hemodialyzed patients may cause bone calcium resorption, resulting in even more severe hypercalcemia.\
**Answer:** osteocalcin

5. Calcium gluconate, in fact, can potentially contribute greater than 80% of the total ____________ load from TPN and the accumulation of this element in bone may be the etiology of metabolic bone pain.\
**Answer:** aluminum

6. ___________ is the only pediatric amino acid formula that contains significant amounts of cysteine (about 15 mg/dL).\
**Answer:** TrophAmine®

7. ___ appears to be the primary determinant of calcium/phosphate solubility.\
**Answer:** pH

8. _____________ increases serum calcium concentration by promoting osteoclastic activity and calcium resorption from bone, by increasing proximal renal tubular calcium resorption, and by enhancing gastrointestinal tract absorption of calcium through formation of 1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol and Ca-binding protein.\
**Answer:** parathormone or parathyroid hormone

9. Most patients with hypocalcemia have a ____ (low,high) intracellular magnesium content.\
**Answer:** low

10. _____% of oral calcium is absorbed from the small intestine (duodenum and proximal jejunum)\
**Answer:** 30

11. The RDA for pregnant (13 weeks or more) and lactating women is based on __% bioavailability.\
**Answer:** 50

12. Once calcium gains entry into the systemic circulation it becomes ___% - ___% protein-bound by albumin.\
**Answer:** 40 - 50

13. ________ (hypercalcemia, hypocalcemia)  presents as a shortened QT interval with an ST segment shift.\
**Answer:** hypercalcemia

14. Ionized calcium represents approximately __% -__% of plasma calcium while the remainder is primarily bound to albumin.\
**Answer:** 50-60

15. Approximately 15% of critically ill surgical patients develop __________, which may represent a new form of hyperparathyroidism.\
**Answer:** hypercalcemia

16. The product of serum calcium and serum phosphate is used as a clinical guide in the assessment of soft tissue calcium/phosphate deposits (when the product exceeds ___ - ____mg/dL) and defective mineralization of bone (when the product is below 30-40 mg/dL).\
**Answer:** 60-70

17. The circadian acrophase (highest point of the day) of total calcium, as well as actual (ionized) calcium, is approximately ____ pm, while circadian nadirs are at about 6:00 am and 2:00 am\
**Answer:** 12:00 Noon

18. Serum from both normal subjects and critically ill patients contained an ether-extractable factor which _________(lowered, raised) ionized calcium concentrations and increased albumin-calcium binding.\
**Answer:** lowered

19. Lipid infusions have _________(lowered, raised) mean serum ionized calcium values in critically ill patients.\
**Answer:** lowered

20. The best clinical evaluation of ionized calcium is achieved by taking measurements from _____ _____ _____ _____ (sample fluid).\
**Answer:** anaerobically collected whole blood

21. Approximately ____% of filtered calcium is reabsorbed from the kidney, while less than 1% is excreted.\
**Answer:** 99

22. Increased _________ (about 20% greater than continuous) occurred with the use of cyclic TPN (75% of cyclic TPN patients were in negative calcium balance).\
**Answer:** hypercalciuria

23. Hypercalciuria induced by high levels of amino acid infusion during TPN may contribute to the development of _____ ____ ______.\
**Answer:** metabolic bone disease

24. Since cardiac function is intimately related to the calcium cation, intravenous calcium solutions should be administered at a rate no greater than ___-___ mEq/min.\
**Answer:** 0.75-3

25. Dietary phosphorus has been observed to have a __________ effect.\
**Answer:** hypocalciuretic

26. TPN-induced hypercalciuria can be attenuated in the short term by intravenous _____________.\
**Answer:** phosphate

27. Metabolic bone disease is characterized by ________, ________, _________ and _____ _____.\
**Answer:** hypercalcemia, hypercalciuria, osteomalacia and bone pain

28. Calcium/phosphate salts deposit in soft tissues more frequently in the face of ________and occur predictably at levels of serum calcium concentrations of 17 mg/dL or higher.\
**Answer:** hypercalcemia

29. ________ calcium intake has been associated with hypertension.\
**Answer:** reduced

30. __________'s phenomenon is elicited by applying a blood pressure cuff to an upper extremity and inflating the cuff above the systolic blood pressure for three minutes.\
**Answer:** Trousseau

31. __________'s sign identifies tetany as a unique lateral contraction of the facial muscles and the muscles of the eyelids elicited by percussion of the facial nerve anterior to the ear.\
**Answer:** Chvostek

32. Cardiac standstill can occur at plasma calcium levels of greater than ___ mg/dL.\
**Answer:** 18

33. Elevated chloride to phosphate ratios beyond 33 have been associated with the ___________of primary hyperparathyroidism.\
**Answer:** hypercalcemia

34. __________ (acidemia, alkalemia) causes calciuria.\
**Answer:** acidemia

35. Although more chemically reactive, replacement of calcium gluconate by calcium chloride may help to reduce the aluminum content by approximately ___ % in the final parenteral solution\
**Answer:** 34

36. Adding ___________ (alkalinizers, acidifiers) to total parenteral nutrition solutions has been associated with less urinary calcium excretion.\
**Answer:** alkalinizers
